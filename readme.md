# Radar Interference Reduction in Airspace: A New Breakthrough! Research on MIMO-FMCW Radar Interference Suppression Technology [with MATLAB Code]

I. Title: MIMO-FMCW Vehicle Radar Spatial Interference Reduction
II. Abstract: This paper investigates the issue of inter-vehicle radar interference suppression using Frequency Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) signals and Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) virtual arrays in vehicle-mounted radar systems. It is the first to derive a spatial interference signal model that considers both temporal and frequency non-coherentities (e.g., different FMCW parameters and time offsets), as well as the slow time MIMO coding non-coherency between the victim radar and the interferer radar, along with differences in array configurations. By integrating the derived interference signal model with the standard MIMO-FMCW target signal model, the problem of interference suppression under non-coherent MIMO-FMCW interference is transformed into a spatial target detection problem. Leveraging the structural characteristics of the interference model in the transmit and receive steering vector spaces, a detector is derived through beamforming optimization, and an adaptive version is further proposed to enhance its practical applicability. Performance evaluations through closed-form expressions, synthesis-level simulations, and system-level simulations indicate that the proposed detector outperforms the selected benchmark method in terms of detection performance.
III. Introduction: Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) and Autonomous Driving require high-resolution environmental perception systems capable of reliable detection and identification of stationary targets (such as buildings and trees) and dynamic targets (like vehicles and pedestrians) under various weather conditions. Compared to other sensors such as cameras and LiDAR, radar can operate reliably in adverse weather and nighttime conditions, and at lower cost and processing overhead.
Current automotive radar widely adopts Frequency Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) technology, which allows for low-sample rate reception design while utilizing a wide sweeping bandwidth to achieve high distance resolution. However, due to poor angle resolution (especially in elevation), FMCW radar is limited in high-resolution perception tasks. To improve angle resolution, radar chip manufacturers employ various schemes to construct large apertures to form high-directivity beams. Among these, MIMO radar forms a large virtual array through fewer transmit (Tx) and receive (Rx) antennas and an RF link, achieving commercial applications that can provide dozens or even hundreds of virtual channels in azimuth and elevation directions.
With the widespread application of MIMO-FMCW automotive radar (such as Texas Instruments' AWR chipset), interference issues within co-channel bands such as 76-81GHz are becoming increasingly prominent. Interference between radars can be categorized into coherent interference and non-coherent interference: coherent interference occurs when the received waveforms and array parameters of the victim radar and the interferer radar are identical, leading to false targets; non-coherent interference arises from parameter differences, causing the noise floor of the victim radar to increase and is more common.
Interference suppression methods can be divided into transmission end scheduling (needs coordination between radars and additional communication bandwidth), transmission end waveform randomization (suitable for coherent interference), and receiver end processing. Research has been conducted in the fast time domain, slow time domain, and range-Doppler domain on receiver end processing, but there is a lack of clear interference signal models in the spatial domain for MIMO-FMCW radar, making it difficult to fully utilize the spatial structure of interference. This paper first constructs a target detection model for MIMO-FMCW interference in the spatial domain, proposing a new detector to address the limitations of existing methods.
IV. Method Introduction
1. Signal Model Construction• Target Signal Model: The victim radar uses an uniform linear array (ULA) for both transmission and reception, where the target signal exhibits a Kronecker product structure between the emission and reception vectors in the spatial domain, obtained through distance-Doppler Fourier transform (FFT) and MIMO waveform separation.
1. Interference Signal Model: Considering the differences in FMCW parameters, time offsets, MIMO coding, and array configuration between the interfered radar and the victim radar, it is deduced that the interference signals in the spatial domain also have Kronecker structure, but the decoded transmitted steering vectors are subject to various non-coherent factors after decoding.
2. Detection Problem Modeling: The detection of space-domain targets is transformed into a composite hypothesis test problem: the null hypothesis (H₀) is that the received signal only contains interference and noise, and the alternative hypothesis (H₁) is that the received signal contains targets, interference, and noise.
3. Non-Adaptive Generalized Subspace (GS) Detector: Based on the structure of the transmit and receive steering vectors for interference, this detector designs an optimization objective for beamforming. It suppresses interference while maintaining the detection gain of targets. This detector utilizes statistical information of interference power through regularization projection matrices, and theoretical analysis shows that its detection performance is superior to baseline detectors that only use receiver-side information.
4. Adaptive Generalized Subspace (AGS) Detector: Addressing the issue of difficultly precisely estimating interference parameters in actual scenarios, this detector reconstructs the covariance matrix of interference using Capon space spectrum estimation without explicitly estimating the number of interferers, steering vectors, and power. By utilizing samples from adjacent range - Doppler cells, this method adaptively reconstructs the covariance matrix of interference, enhancing robustness in practical applications.
5. Performance Verification Method: Using synthetic data simulations and system-level simulations (based on MATLAB phased array system toolbox), the detection performance is evaluated through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, angle spectra, and output interference power (OIP) cumulative distribution function (CDF).
V. Conclusion This paper focuses on the issue of inter-interference suppression in non-coherent MIMO-FMCW vehicle radars. By deriving a clear non-coherent interference signal model, the problem of interference suppression is transformed into a spatial target detection issue. Non-adaptive GS detectors utilizing information from the interfered transmitter are proposed, as well as AGS detectors based on adaptive reconstruction of the covariance matrix from the interfered structure. The analytical and empirical evaluations through synthetic data and system-level simulations demonstrate that the proposed detectors have superior interference suppression capabilities and target detection performance compared to various benchmark methods such as receive subspace detectors and LCMV-SMI detectors.
